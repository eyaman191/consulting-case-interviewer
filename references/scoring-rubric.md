# Case Interview Scoring Rubric

## Dimensions (score each 1–5, or N/O if not observed)

| Dimension | Weight | What strong performance looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem framing and clarification | 10% | Defines decision, objective, scope, constraints; clarifies only high-value unknowns |
| Structuring and hypothesis | 20% | Tailored issue tree, prioritized hypotheses, useful test plan, covers material causes |
| Quantitative reasoning | 20% | Correct equations, units, mental math, sensitivities, economic interpretation |
| Business judgment and creativity | 20% | Insightful customer/market logic, causal mechanisms, differentiated and feasible choices |
| Synthesis and recommendation | 20% | Answer first, evidence, trade-offs, actionable next steps, concise executive communication |
| Communication and interview leadership | 10% | Clear spoken signposting, active listening, confident but revisable stance, efficient responses |

Scores: **1** = substantial gaps; **2** = inconsistent; **3** = solid with notable improvement areas; **4** = strong; **5** = exceptional. If fewer than four dimensions have been observed, do not provide a reliable overall score; report provisional dimension feedback instead. Hints do not automatically fail a candidate, but should be disclosed under independence.

For full interviews, calculate weighted total out of 5, using observed dimensions and renormalizing their weights only if needed. Avoid false precision: display one decimal place and indicate when evidence is limited. Overall descriptors: 1–1.9 early-stage; 2–2.9 developing; 3–3.9 interview-ready in some areas; 4–5 strong performance in this mock interview. These are practice descriptors, not hiring probabilities.

## Debrief format

1. **Bottom line:** overall judgment in 2 sentences; score if sufficiently observed.
2. **Scorecard:** dimension / 1–5 or N/O / specific observed evidence.
3. **Strongest moment:** point to a concrete candidate behavior.
4. **Most important gap:** explain the reasoning failure and business consequence.
5. **Better answer:** show the revised 2–5 sentence version the candidate could have given at that moment.
6. **Next drill:** one exercise targeting the highest-leverage weakness, with a success criterion.

Be candid and specific. Never give generic praise, and do not infer characteristics such as confidence or spoken eloquence from a text transcript alone.
