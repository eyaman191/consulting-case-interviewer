# Case Blueprints and Calibration

These are seeds, not fixed answer keys. Build a coherent hidden case ledger before presenting any case.

## Blueprint A: Repositioning a retail brand (medium)
A regional coffee chain is losing profit while store traffic is steady. Decide whether to premiumize, change mix, expand, or pursue an operational fix. Quant exhibit: orders × average ticket × gross margin − labor − occupancy. Twist: flagship premium products attract attention but drive very low contribution after added labor.

## Blueprint B: Expansion with infrastructure bottleneck (hard)
A subscription cloud-computing company has strong demand but limited server capacity. Decide whether and how fast to invest in more capacity. Quant exhibit: utilization, unit contribution, payback, financing costs, churn, cash runway. Twist: demand forecasts differ across segments and over-provisioning harms cash flow. Test whether demand, finance, or operations is the true binding constraint.

## Blueprint C: Event economics (hard)
An entertainment promoter wants to attract high-profile international acts while making the fan experience profitable. Decide an offer and commercial model. Quant exhibit: capacity, ticket mix, sponsorship, artist guarantees, variable costs. Twist: bundling is easy for rivals to imitate; differentiation must come from a distinctive capability, partnership, or customer experience.

## Blueprint D: Consumer app growth (hard)
A social discovery app helps people make plans at nearby places, but new users encounter low content density. Decide launch city, activation loop, and monetization path. Quant exhibit: active users per neighborhood, event supply, activation, retention, invite conversion, acquisition cost. Twist: a growth tactic boosts sign-ups without generating successful real-world meetups.

## Blueprint E: Margin decline (easy-to-medium)
A consumer product business has rising revenue and falling profit. Diagnose price, mix, volume, COGS, channel cost, and overhead. Quant exhibit: two years of segment revenue and contribution margins. Twist: fastest-growing segment destroys margin due to return rates.

## Difficulty tuning

- **Easy:** one issue, transparent arithmetic, 2–3 buckets, simple recommendation.
- **Medium:** multiple competing hypotheses, one data exhibit, one calculation, one trade-off.
- **Hard:** ambiguous objective, conflicting evidence, second-order effects, multi-step economics, defensibility, implementation constraints, and counterfactuals.

## Case integrity checklist

Before asking the opening question, make sure all disclosed numbers are mathematically consistent. Preserve a log of numbers given, timing, and candidate decisions in the active conversation. If a contradiction occurs, acknowledge and correct it rather than retroactively rewriting the case.
